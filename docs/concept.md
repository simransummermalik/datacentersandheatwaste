# Concept and research questions

This repository tests a narrow engineering question: can a useful portion of
data-center cooling heat be recovered at a temperature and reliability that a
greenhouse can use?

It also keeps a separate siting question in view: if the thermal connection is
technically workable, are there places where a greenhouse connected to that
infrastructure could support useful local production? “Useful” is intentionally
not the same as “solves food insecurity.” A community’s food access depends on
price, transportation, land, ownership, distribution, crop choice, and resident
priorities as well as on how much a greenhouse can grow.

The model therefore reports thermal power, peak greenhouse area, mismatch, and
storage—not people fed or a claimed reduction in food insecurity.

## Timeline

- March 2026 — original concept and initial question.
- October 2026 — public repository and expanded computational analysis.

## Boundaries

The steady-state model covers IT load, heat collection, a heat exchanger, a
temperature-lift option, distribution loss, and a sensible water-storage
screen. The greenhouse load covers transmission and infiltration at a stated
design temperature. It does not select equipment, guarantee crop yield, or
replace a site energy audit. A synthetic one-day profile demonstrates mismatch;
it is not a measured weather year.
